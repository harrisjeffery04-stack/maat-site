MATERIAL INJUSTICE SCREENER
working name — final naming at the partner org's discretion

The screener whose headline feature is a correct no.

Point it at an intake queue. It tells you which case files carry a structural habeas ground worth a lawyer pulling — and, just as usefully, which don't.

Note on naming & affiliation. This is an independent tool built by an expert-witness-network-connected developer. It is offered to post-conviction and wrongful-conviction review organizations for their evaluation; it does not imply affiliation with or endorsement by any organization unless and until that organization agrees in writing.

1.Reads the record

Deterministic analysis of a case record. No LLM in the read path — the same input always yields the same result.

2.Flags the drift

Names the rule provisions apt to drift below the federal habeas floor, with the exact forcing authority — Martinez/Trevino, Coleman, Lee v. Kemna.

3.Or abstains

Where the text carries no drift marker, it says so. A correct abstention spends zero attorney time — that is the point.

How it's meant to be used

Each lead is an input to a lawyer's judgment, never a substitute: here is a rule provision whose wording is apt to drift below the federal habeas floor, and the exact authority that forces the question — you decide which files to pull. The tool screens; licensed counsel decides. It offers no verdict and no prediction of any outcome.

Important. This is a research and triage screening tool. It surfaces structural legal grounds for review by licensed attorneys and qualified legal organizations. It does not give legal advice, does not predict whether any conviction will be overturned, and is not a law firm or a substitute for legal representation. Every flagged lead requires independent verification by a licensed attorney. Screening accuracy is reported at its reproducible, tested tier and describes the underlying gate's precision on a labeled research corpus, not a probability of exoneration in any individual case.
